Perspectives

Where we stand

Positions we hold, stated plainly, because they explain how an engagement with us will actually run.

01 — Diagnostics

Most turnaround plans fail at the diagnosis, not the execution.

By the time a management team commissions help, there is usually already a theory in the room about what is wrong — and the work quietly becomes an exercise in confirming it. The expensive failure is not a badly executed plan. It is a well-executed plan aimed at a symptom.

This is why our diagnostic starts from the operating data rather than the leadership interview, and why root-cause analysis is a named deliverable rather than an implicit step.

Pillar 01 — Strategy & Consultancy

02 — Delivery

The hand-off is where the value leaks.

Strategy firm to build partner to raise adviser: three transfers, each one a chance for context to be lost and for accountability to become negotiable. Every transfer also costs a discovery phase, paid for again.

Holding all three is not an upsell argument. It removes the two most expensive moments in the growth journey.

How the pillars connect

03 — Operations

Outsourcing is a control decision before it is a cost decision.

The question is never simply whether a function is cheaper elsewhere. It is whether the judgement inside that function is a source of advantage. Where it is, co-source and keep the judgement in-house. Where it is not, hand the whole function over and hold it to a service level.

Getting this backwards — outsourcing the judgement and keeping the administration — is the common and expensive mistake.

Managed Operations

04 — Applied AI

Automate the process you have already fixed.

Automation applied to a broken process produces a faster broken process and a new integration to maintain. Applied AI is worth deploying where the workflow is understood, the data is trustworthy, and the cost or cycle time being removed can be measured before and after.

Everywhere else it is a pilot, and pilots should be priced and timeboxed as such.

Services & Technology

05 — Capital

Fundability is an operating condition, not a document.

A raise process exposes the operating model to people paid to find the flaw in it. No narrative survives a monthly close that cannot be reconciled, or a metric that changes definition between the deck and the data room.

Which is why our readiness assessment can conclude that a business should not go to market yet — and say what would have to change first.

Pillar 03 — Capital

06 — Regulated sectors

In regulated businesses, compliance is the architecture.

Treating the control environment as a review gate at the end of a build is the reliable way to discover, late, that the product cannot ship. In clinical and compliance-heavy work the regulatory perimeter is a design input alongside the user need — it shapes the data model, the audit trail and the release process.
